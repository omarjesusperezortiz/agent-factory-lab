Stage Show: a design skill that records its own demo and checks every frame

Benji Taylor, head of design at X, wrote down how he builds the animated product demos on landing pages and packaged it as a skill called Stage Show. It's a seven-step process, not a list of rules. The step no other design skill has: the agent records the demo while it plays, lays every moment out on one sheet, and a script checks that sheet before the agent can hand the demo over. Running example: the beat-maker app from the video, and its one demo action, "add a drum track".

What this means for us

Records the animation and checks a sheet of frames taken during the motionOur visual-explainer check waits for a scene to finish, then takes one screenshot. That's the "screenshot once everything has stopped" check the video calls blind to motion.Capture 8–10 frames while each scene runs, tile them into one image, and check that image for jumps and text that overflows.
Can't hand over until the check passes; fixes the biggest problem first, then records againVerification is a checklist step. Nothing blocks publishing.Make the frame-sheet check a gate in the explainer flow: fail, fix the worst frame, record again.
Writes rehearsal.md next to the demo: the example it started from, what it changed, what it checkedExplainers ship with no record of what was checked.Leave a short check note per explainer (scenes run, widths tested, issues fixed).
Starts from one of ~40 real demos instead of rules aloneThe skill says "never reuse an earlier stage", so every explainer starts blank.Keep a small gallery of good past scenes as references to learn from, not to copy.
Plain-words feedback gets written back into the skillCorrections land in memory or CLAUDE.md now and then.After each review round, ask the agent to add the fix to the skill as a rule.
